WHAT IS A CLAUSE?
▪A clause is a group of words that contains:
▪a subject
▪a verb
▪Example:
▪She plays tennis.
▪Subject → She
▪Verb → plays
▪Clauses are the building blocks of sentences.
Type
Meaning
Independent Clause
Can stand alone as a sentence
Dependent Clause
Cannot stand alone

--- Page 3 ---
INDEPENDENT CLAUSE (MAIN CLAUSE)
▪An independent clause expresses a complete idea.
▪Structure:
▪Subject + Verb + Complete Meaning
▪Examples:
▪She went home. 
▪They are studying. 
▪I like coffee. 
▪The baby is sleeping. 
▪These are complete sentences.

--- Page 4 ---
DEPENDENT CLAUSE (SUBORDINATE CLAUSE)
▪A dependent clause does NOT express a complete idea.
▪It needs an independent clause.
▪Examples:
▪because I was tired 
▪when she arrived 
▪if it rains 
▪These are NOT complete sentences.
▪Complete Sentences:
▪I slept early because I was tired. 
▪We left when she arrived. 
▪We will stay home if it rains.

--- Page 5 ---
TYPES OF DEPENDENT CLAUSES
▪There are 3 main types:
▪Adjective Clauses 
▪Adverb Clauses 
▪Noun Clauses

--- Page 6 ---
ADJECTIVE CLAUSES (RELATIVE CLAUSES)
▪Describe a noun.
▪Usually begin with: who / whom / whose / which / that 
▪Examples:
▪The man who lives next door is kind. 
▪I like the book that you gave me. 
▪The girl who won the prize is my friend. 
▪This is the phone which I bought yesterday. 
▪The clause gives more information about a noun.

--- Page 7 ---
ADVERB CLAUSES
▪Function: Give information about:  time / reason / condition / contrast 
▪Often begin with: because / when / if / although / since / while / as / 
unless…  
▪Examples:
▪I stayed home because I was sick. 
▪Call me when you arrive. 
▪If it rains, we will cancel the trip. 
▪Although he was tired, he continued working. 
▪The clause explains why, when, how, or under what condition.

--- Page 8 ---
NOUN CLAUSES
▪Function: Act like a noun.
▪They can be a:  subject  / object / complement 
▪Often begin with: that / what / whether / if / wh- words 
▪Examples:
▪What she said surprised everyone. 
▪I know that he is honest. 
▪She asked whether we were ready. 
▪What you did was amazing. 
▪The whole clause acts like a noun.

--- Page 9 ---
COMPOUND SENTENCES
▪Structure:
▪Two independent clauses join together.
▪Joining words:
▪and 
▪but 
▪or 
▪so 
▪yet 
▪Examples:
▪I was tired, but I finished my homework. 
▪She cooked dinner, and he washed the dishes. 
▪We can stay home, or we can go out.

--- Page 10 ---
COMPLEX SENTENCES
▪Structure:
▪One independent clause + one dependent clause
▪Examples:
▪Because it was raining, we stayed inside. 
▪I called him after I arrived home. 
▪Although she was nervous, she spoke well.

--- Page 11 ---
COMPOUND-COMPLEX SENTENCES
▪Structure:
▪Two independent clauses + one dependent clause
▪Examples:
▪I wanted to go outside, but it was raining because the storm had 
started. 
▪She cooked dinner, and he cleaned the kitchen while the children 
studied.

--- Page 12 ---
PRACTICE
▪Identify the clause type:
▪Because I was late, I took a taxi. 
▪The boy who is wearing blue is my brother. 
▪What he said surprised me. 
▪She was tired, but she continued studying.
Answers:
1.Adverb clause 
2.Adjective clause 
3.Noun clause 
4.Compound sentence

WHAT IS AN ADJECTIVE CLAUSE?
▪An adjective clause (relative clause) gives more information 
about a noun.
▪It works like an adjective.
▪It usually comes after the noun it modifies.
▪Example:
▪The girl who is wearing a red dress is my sister. 
▪The clause describes the girl.

--- Page 3 ---
STRUCTURE
▪Noun + relative clause
▪Relative words:
▪who 
▪whom 
▪whose 
▪which 
▪that 
▪where
▪… noun + relative word + S + verb + C …
▪… noun + relative word + verb + C…

MODIFYING THE SUBJECT
▪The adjective clause can describe the subject of the sentence.
▪Examples:
▪The student who studies every night gets excellent grades. 
▪The movie that we watched was exciting. 
▪The teacher who teaches us English is friendly. 
▪The house which stands on the corner is very old. 
▪The restaurant where we ate dinner was crowded. 
▪The man whose son lives abroad works here. 
▪The clauses describe the subjects:
student, movie, teacher, house, restaurant, man

--- Page 6 ---
MODIFYING THE OBJECT
▪The adjective clause can also describe the object.
▪Examples:
▪I met the woman whose husband is a doctor. 
▪She bought the car that was parked outside. 
▪We visited the teacher who helped us last year. 
▪I know the boy who won the competition. 
▪They watched the movie which won an award. 
▪He sold the house that was near the park. 
▪The clauses describe the objects:
woman, car, teacher, boy, movie, house

--- Page 7 ---
WHO / WHOM
▪WHO → subject / WHOM → object
▪Examples:
▪The girl who called me is my cousin. 
▪The man whom we visited was very kind. 
▪Note:
▪In everyday English, whom is often replaced by who.
▪Common spoken English:
▪The man who we visited was very kind.

--- Page 8 ---
WHOSE
▪WHOSE shows possession.
▪Whose + noun - always
▪Examples:
▪I know the girl whose brother is a pilot. 
▪The man whose wallet was lost called the police. 
▪The student whose phone rang was embarrassed.
▪Wrong – whose + verb/ subject pronoun

--- Page 9 ---
WHICH / THAT
▪WHICH → things
▪THAT → people or things
▪
Examples:
▪The book which I borrowed is excellent. 
▪The cake that she made was delicious. 
▪The car that he bought is very fast. 
▪The computer which stopped working is old.

--- Page 10 ---
WHERE
▪WHERE is used for places.
▪…where + subject /noun + verb + C…
▪Examples:
▪The school where I studied is very large. 
▪This is the café where we first met. 
▪The city where he lives is beautiful.
▪…where + verb – this is wrong

--- Page 11 ---
IMPORTANT POSITION RULE
▪The adjective clause comes immediately after the noun it 
modifies.
▪
Correct:
▪The boy who is wearing glasses is my cousin. 
▪
Incorrect:
▪Who is wearing glasses the boy is my cousin.

--- Page 12 ---
SHORTENED RELATIVE CLAUSES
▪Sometimes we can shorten the clause.
▪Examples:
▪The man standing near the door is my uncle.
(= The man who is standing near the door…) 
▪The book written by Tom is popular.
(= The book that was written by Tom…)

WHAT IS OMISSION?
▪Omission = leaving out a relative pronoun.
▪Sometimes we can remove:
▪who 
▪whom 
▪which 
▪that

--- Page 3 ---
WHEN CAN WE OMIT?
▪We can omit the relative pronoun when it is the OBJECT
of the clause.
▪Examples:
▪The man (who/whom) I saw yesterday was friendly. 
▪The car (that/which) I bought is very fast. 
▪The girl (whom/that) we invited came late. 
▪The movie (that/which) we watched was exciting. 

--- Page 4 ---
WHEN CANNOT WE OMIT?
▪We CANNOT omit the pronoun when it is the SUBJECT
of the clause.
▪Examples:
▪The man who robbed the bank was arrested. 
▪The girl who sits next to me is very smart. 
▪The students who study hard get good grades. 
▪The dog that barked all night was noisy. 
▪Incorrect:
▪The man robbed the bank was arrested. 
▪The girl sits next to me is very smart.

--- Page 5 ---
EASY WAY TO CHECK
▪Ask:
▪“Does the clause already have a subject?”
▪Example:
▪The man who I saw yesterday is not here now. 
→ subject = I
→ who = object
can omit 
▪Example:
▪The man who called me yesterday is not here now. 
→ who = subject
cannot omit 

--- Page 6 ---
USE OF “THAT”
▪After these words, we usually use THAT:
▪all 
▪everything 
▪little 
▪much 
▪none 
▪no 
▪superlatives (best, biggest, most beautiful…)

--- Page 7 ---
EXAMPLES WITH “THAT”
▪Everything that he said was true. 
▪All the books that I bought are useful. 
▪This is the best movie that I have ever seen. 
▪She told me little that was helpful. 
▪That is the most interesting story that I know.

--- Page 8 ---
RELATIVE PRONOUNS AFTER PREPOSITIONS
▪When the preposition comes at the END,
▪the pronoun can be omitted.
▪Examples:
▪The man (who/whom/that) I spoke to was helpful. 
▪The friend (who/whom/that) I traveled with speaks French. 
▪The chair (which/that) I sat on was broken. 
▪Omitted:
▪The man I spoke to was helpful. 
▪The friend I traveled with speaks French. 
▪The chair I sat on was broken.

--- Page 9 ---
PREPOSITION BEFORE RELATIVE PRONOUN
▪If the preposition comes BEFORE the pronoun:
▪USE:
▪whom 
▪which 
▪NOT:
▪who 
▪that 
▪Because who/that cannot be omitted

--- Page 10 ---

▪The man to whom I spoke was helpful. who
▪The friend with whom I traveled spoke French. who
▪The chair on which I sat was uncomfortable. that
▪Less Formal / Everyday English:
▪The man I spoke to was helpful. 
▪The friend I traveled with spoke French. 
▪The chair I sat on was uncomfortable.

DEFINING & NON-DEFINING 
RELATIVE CLAUSES

--- Page 2 ---
DEFINING RELATIVE CLAUSE
Gives important information, without it, the sentence meaning 
changes or becomes unclear.
❌No commas
Examples:
The students who studied hard passed the exam. 
The boy who is wearing a blue jacket is my brother. 
The book that I bought yesterday is interesting. 
The people who live next door are friendly. 
We need the clause to know exactly which people/things we mean.

--- Page 3 ---
NON-DEFINING RELATIVE CLAUSE
Gives extra information. We already know who or what we are 
talking about.
✅Use commas
Examples:
Mr. Ali, who teaches English, is very patient. 
Paris, which is famous for art, attracts many tourists. 
My sister, who lives in Korea, is visiting us next month. 
The Nile River, which is the longest river in Africa, is very 
important. 
If we remove the clause, the main meaning stays the same.

--- Page 4 ---
IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE
✔Defining:
The travelers who knew about the flood took another road. 
Only SOME travelers knew.
✔Non-defining:
The travelers, who knew about the flood, took another road. 
ALL travelers knew.

--- Page 5 ---
COMMAS CHANGE MEANING
✔Defining:
The boys who wanted to play football were disappointed. 
Only some boys wanted football.
✔Non-defining:
The boys, who wanted to play football, were disappointed. 
All the boys wanted football.

MORE DEFINING EXAMPLES

The students who arrived late missed the test. 
The restaurant which serves Italian food is crowded. 
The woman whose car was stolen called the police.

--- Page 9 ---

Examples:
Tashkent, which is the capital of Uzbekistan, is very

Steve Jobs, who founded Apple, changed technology. 
My uncle, whose house is near the river, loves fishing. 
Ann, whom I met at university, works in Japan now.

--- Page 10 ---
OMITTING THE CLAUSE
In non-defining clauses, removing the clause does NOT 
change the main meaning.
✔Example:
These books, which you can buy anywhere, are useful. 
Without clause:
These books are useful. 
Main meaning stays the same.

--- Page 11 ---
“OF WHOM / OF WHICH”
Used in formal English.
Helps:
avoid repetition
connect ideas smoothly
sound academic/formal
Structure:
some of whom 
most of whom 
both of whom 
several of which 
many of which

I met many students, several of whom spoke Korean. 
She has two sons, both of whom work abroad. 
He invited ten people, few of whom arrived on time. 
We visited a village, many of whose houses were damaged.

noun + of which / of whom
Used for:
result

name

roof 
title

5. Predlogning o'rni 
Predlog ikki joyda tura oladi: relative olmosh oldida (rasmiy) yoki ergash gap oxirida (og'zaki). 
▸  The colleague with whom I shared an office has retired.  — rasmiy / yozma 
▸  The colleague (who / that) I shared an office with has retired.  — neytral / og'zaki 
Qat'iy qoidalar 
•  Predlogdan keyin faqat whom va which keladi. 
✗  the man to who I spoke   /   the theory on that it rests 
✓  the man to whom I spoke   /   the theory on which it rests 
•  Predlog oldinga chiqsa, olmoshni tushirib bo'lmaydi va that ishlatilmaydi. 
•  Phrasal verb bo'lsa, predlog odatda oxirida qoladi: the problem (that) we have to deal with.

8. Sentential which — butun gapga ishora 
which oldingi butun gapga ishora qilishi mumkin. Bunda that ham, what ham ishlatilmaydi, vergul esa majburiy. 
▸  She passed with distinction, which surprised nobody. 
▸  The funding was cut in March, which explains the delay. 
✗  She passed with distinction, what surprised nobody. 
✗  She passed with distinction that surprised nobody. 
9. that va which — qaysi biri afzal? 
that afzal ko'riladi: 
•  all, everything, nothing, anything, something, little, much dan keyin 
•  the only, the very, the first, the last, the next dan keyin 
•  orttirma daraja (superlative) dan keyin 
•  cleft gaplarda: It was John that told me. 
▸  Everything that could go wrong did go wrong. 
▸  She was the only candidate that mentioned the environment. 
which MAJBURIY: 
•  non-defining (vergulli) gaplarda, •  predlogdan keyin, •  butun gapga ishora qilganda
